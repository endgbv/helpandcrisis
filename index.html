🚨 if you are in immediate danger

get somewhere safer first.

  1. Move toward other people, a shop, security desk, neighbour, hospital or another safer place.
  2. If you are in immediate danger, call the fastest emergency service available. 112 works from a mobile phone. 10111 is the SAPS emergency number.
  3. Tell them your location first, then what is happening, whether anyone has a weapon and whether anyone is injured.
  4. Do not confront or chase the person if that could make things worse.

🩺 if you've been sexually assaulted

  • Get somewhere safe and seek medical care as soon as you can.
  • A Thuthuzela Care Centre can provide medical, psychosocial and justice-related support.
  • If you may want forensic evidence collected, avoid washing, bathing or changing clothes if you can safely avoid doing so. If you already have, still get medical care.
  • Keep messages, photographs and other evidence if it is safe to do so.
  • You can ask Women For Change or the GBV Command Centre to help you work out your next step.

🛡️ if someone is attacking you

Forget movie-style self-defence. The goal is not to win the fight - it is to create a chance to escape.

1. get away first

  • Move toward people, security, a shop, reception or another safer place.
  • Put a door, car, counter, table or other solid object between you and the attacker.
  • Shout clearly: “HELP. CALL THE POLICE.” or call someone by name if you can.
  • If you fall, protect your head and neck and look for a chance to get up and move away.

2. don't rely on these

  • Scratching for DNA: don't stop to collect DNA during an attack. Getting away is more important. Evidence can be collected afterward.
  • Kneeing the groin: it can fail, especially if the attacker is holding you or is positioned differently than expected. Don't depend on it as your plan.
  • Punching the throat: this is difficult to land accurately under stress and can cause serious injury. Don't treat it as a guaranteed “off switch.”
  • There is no single magic move that reliably stops a larger or stronger attacker.

3. if you are trapped

  • Use whatever movement helps you break contact and make space.
  • Push away, move around an obstacle, pull free and run when an opening appears.
  • If you need to use physical force, focus on creating an opportunity to escape rather than continuing the fight.
  • If there is a weapon, prioritise distance, barriers and escape. Do not make trying to disarm someone your first plan.

4. after you get away

  • Get somewhere safe and contact someone you trust.
  • Get medical attention if you are injured.
  • Write down what happened while you remember it: time, place, threats, injuries, witnesses and any weapon involved.
  • If you want forensic evidence collected, seek medical care as soon as possible. Do not delay getting help because you have already washed or changed clothes.

the legal basics

South African law recognises private defence against an unlawful attack that has started or is immediately imminent. The force used must be directed at stopping the attack and must be necessary and reasonable in the circumstances. There is no fixed rule such as “three hits” or a particular body part being legally allowed.

Once the immediate danger has ended, stop. Chasing or hurting someone as revenge is different from defending yourself during an attack.

Remember: escape is the win. You do not have to overpower someone to survive an attack.

Legal information: South African Department of Justice materials explain that private defence applies to an attack that has commenced or is immediately imminent and that defensive force must be necessary and reasonable in the circumstances.

📱 stalking or threats

  • Do not meet the person alone if you feel unsafe.
  • Tell someone you trust what is happening.
  • Save threatening messages, screenshots, call records, usernames and photographs.
  • Write down dates, times, places and witnesses.
  • If they know where you live, work or study, tell relevant security or staff.
  • If the threat is happening now, get to safety and call emergency help.

⚖️ your rights and protection

Protection Order

If someone you have a domestic relationship with is abusing, threatening, stalking or controlling you, you may be able to apply for a Domestic Violence Protection Order at a Magistrates Court.

The Department of Justice says the service is free and emergency services are available 24 hours.

When making a report

Give the location, what happened, when it happened, threats, weapons, injuries, witnesses and any evidence you have.

You can ask a support organisation to help you understand your options before deciding what to do next.

📣 women have been calling for change

Women and advocacy groups have repeatedly called on government to take stronger action against gender-based violence and femicide.

the Women For Change petition

Women For Change launched a Change.org petition calling on the President to declare gender-based violence and femicide a National Disaster. The organisation says the petition received more than 1.1 million signatures. In November 2025, government announced that GBVF would be classified as a National Disaster.

The fight did not end with the declaration. Women For Change says it continues to push for implementation, accountability and practical support for survivors.

view the Change.org petition →
why this is here: safety is not only about what an individual woman can do. It is also about demanding systems that protect women, support survivors and hold perpetrators accountable.

🤝 helping someone else

  • Believe them and listen without blaming them.
  • Ask what they need instead of making decisions for them.
  • Help them get somewhere safe if it is safe for you to do so.
  • Offer to stay with them while they call a support service.
  • Do not confront the alleged abuser if that could put anyone at greater risk.
If there is immediate danger: call 112 from a mobile phone.
