🚨 if you are in immediate danger

get somewhere safer first.

  1. Move toward other people, a shop, security desk, neighbour, hospital or another safer place.
  2. If you are in immediate danger, call the fastest emergency service available. 112 works from a mobile phone. 10111 is the SAPS emergency number.
  3. Tell them your location first, then what is happening, whether anyone has a weapon and whether anyone is injured.
  4. Do not confront or chase the person if that could make things worse.

🩺 if you've been sexually assaulted

  • Get somewhere safe and seek medical care as soon as you can.
  • A Thuthuzela Care Centre can provide medical, psychosocial and justice-related support.
  • If you may want forensic evidence collected, avoid washing, bathing or changing clothes if you can safely avoid doing so. If you already have, still get medical care.
  • Keep messages, photographs and other evidence if it is safe to do so.
  • You can ask Women For Change or the GBV Command Centre to help you work out your next step.

🛡️ if someone is attacking you

your first goal: get away

  • Run toward people or a safer building.
  • Put a door, car, counter or other solid object between you and the attacker.
  • Shout for help.
  • Protect your head, neck and airway.

if you have to defend yourself

  • Create space and break contact so you can escape.
  • Use only the force reasonably necessary to stop the immediate attack and get away.
  • Once the danger has ended, stop. Do not chase or punish the person.

if they have a weapon

  • Do not try to disarm them as your first option.
  • Create distance and use barriers or cover.
  • Run if there is a safe opportunity.

the legal basics

South African law recognises private defence against an actual or imminent unlawful attack. Whether force was lawful depends on the circumstances, including the seriousness of the attack, weapons, available alternatives, the force used and the likely harm.

There is no rule based on the attacker being a man. The same private-defence principles apply regardless of sex.

Simple rule: defend yourself to escape an immediate attack - not to get revenge.

📱 stalking or threats

  • Do not meet the person alone if you feel unsafe.
  • Tell someone you trust what is happening.
  • Save threatening messages, screenshots, call records, usernames and photographs.
  • Write down dates, times, places and witnesses.
  • If they know where you live, work or study, tell relevant security or staff.
  • If the threat is happening now, get to safety and call emergency help.

⚖️ your rights and protection

Protection Order

If someone you have a domestic relationship with is abusing, threatening, stalking or controlling you, you may be able to apply for a Domestic Violence Protection Order at a Magistrates Court.

The Department of Justice says the service is free and emergency services are available 24 hours.

When making a report

Give the location, what happened, when it happened, threats, weapons, injuries, witnesses and any evidence you have.

You can ask a support organisation to help you understand your options before deciding what to do next.

🤝 helping someone else

  • Believe them and listen without blaming them.
  • Ask what they need instead of making decisions for them.
  • Help them get somewhere safe if it is safe for you to do so.
  • Offer to stay with them while they call a support service.
  • Do not confront the alleged abuser if that could put anyone at greater risk.
If there is immediate danger: call 112 from a mobile phone.
